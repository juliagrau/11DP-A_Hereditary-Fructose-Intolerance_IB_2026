### **What is Fructose?**

Fructose is a Monosaccharide found in daily-consumed foods such as fruits, sweeteners, vegetables. Normally consumed but also produced in small amounts by the brain.

### **What is Hereditary Fructose Intolerance?**

HFI is an autosomal recessive disorder provoked by mutations in the isoenzyme Aldolase B. When people with this disorder ingest fructose, there’s accumulations of fructose 1-phosphate and a depletion of ATP. These two consequences lead to abdominal pain, vomit, nausea, hypoglycemia (a drop of blood sugar level) and even kidney and liver failure, which can be life-threatening. This inherited disorder has an estimated population prevalence of 1 in 20000, without sex predilection. 

### **What is aldolase B and what function does it do?**

Aldolase B is one of the three isoenzymes needed for the cleavage of fructose-1 and to catalyze the reversible conversion of fructose-1,6-bisphosphate to glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate and dihydroxyacetone phosphate. It is found in the liver, kidney and intestinal tissue, important organs in fructose metabolism.

### **Why are the accumulations of fructose 1-phosphate harmful?**

The fructose is rapidly converted into fructose 1-phosphate by fructokinase, and without aldolase B to catalyze the molecule, it acummulates. This causes a depletion of both ATP and phosphate, elements responsible for long-term organ injury.

This also leads to an increment of the production of uric acid due to an increase of adenine nucleotide degradation by adenosine deaminase, which has a similar result in the processes seen in glucose-6-phosphatase and crutose 1,6-diphophatase deficiencies. The acummulation of fructose also leads to pyruvate kinase (the enzyme that catalyzes the final step of glycolisis) being activated and thus, accumulation of precursos of the Krebs cycle, that also contribute to acidosis.

### **How is this disease diagnosed and treated?**

The diagnosis of this disease comes after several clinical tests regarding the reaction of the body after the ingestion of fructose, sucrose or foods that contain sorbitol. There is a more invasive method that consists of a liver biopsy to examine the presence of aldolase B.  Urine can also be inspected to observe reducing substances and verify if the values of each of these match the expected results.

The main treatment for HFI is a diet free of fructose and sucrose, including products that contain sorbitol. This includes medicines or vaccines that have sorbitol as a main component.

**In adult patients, do they show a higher preference to follow pharmacological therapy as a complement to their current diet or a replacement of it?**

Different patients of adult age were recruited from clinics and through social media to complete a labeled discrete choice experiment, which was followed by an interview . In any set, they were asked to choose between their current fructose-restricted diet or pharmacological therapy, considering various factors.

After finalizing the experiment, a random parameter model showed that they tended to prefer their diet over the pharmacological therapy. However, most of the patients who participated in the experiment expressed a preference for an option that involved taking a single daily tablet without any fructose restrictions, side effects, or additional costs -an option that was more associated with pharmacological therapy than the fructose-restricted diet.

Thus, the final conclusion of this study was that, despite the initial parameter model indicating that the patients preferred their diet over pharmacological therapy, it ultimately turned out to be the opposite.
